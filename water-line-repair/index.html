Urgent signs

Signs your service line is leaking

A leaking buried line is easy to miss, because the water goes into the soil. Watch for a wet spot or sinkhole between the meter and the house, grass that stays green in one strip, a meter that moves with all fixtures off, a bill that jumped, and low water pressure through the whole house.

A line that surfaces water is an emergency, and so is a line that has dropped pressure to a trickle. Our dispatchers book emergency water line repair 24/7, with same-day and after-hours visits when available. Close the main valve while you wait.

Where the line runs and who owns what

The service line begins at the city's connection and runs through the meter box to your home. The City of Sanger operates the water system, which draws entirely from city wells. Where city responsibility ends and yours begins is a question for Sanger Public Works, and it is worth confirming before any work.

If the leak is on the city side of the meter, the city repairs it. If it is on your side, a licensed plumber does. Call the city if you are not sure.

Diagnosis

Finding a buried leak

The plumber closes the main valve and checks whether the meter stops. A pressure test of the line isolates the section that loses pressure, and listening equipment and a ground microphone find the spot. That precision keeps excavation small, so you pay for a short trench instead of a long one.

For more on the tools, read leak detection, which covers meter tests and hidden leaks inside the house.

Options

Low water pressure

Low pressure throughout the house often traces to the service line, a partly closed valve or a failing pressure regulator, not to the fixtures. A plumber checks static pressure with a gauge at a hose bib and then tests the regulator, the main valve and the line. A line with heavy internal rust restricts flow even with no leak.

If pressure fell suddenly, a leak is likely. If it fell gradually, scale or corrosion is the usual cause. Pressure that is too high is also a problem and shortens the life of fittings and water heaters.

Before any digging

  • Call USA North 811 at least two working days before digging in Fresno County.
  • Keep people and pets away from a surfacing leak and any open trench.
  • Ask the plumber about permits, and check with the Sanger Building Division.
  • Photograph the leak site and the meter for your records.
  • Know where the main valve is and test that it turns.
Local conditions

The water supply in Sanger

Sanger's water is all groundwater from eight wells with an average depth of about 235 feet, treated with carbon filtration and chlorine. The city's 2024 report shows hardness averaging 145.5 ppm. Scale builds up slowly inside old galvanized and copper pipe, and that raises friction and lowers pressure over decades.

Check the age of your service line. If the house is more than 30 years old, like 53 percent of the city's homes according to its housing element, the line may be near the end of its life.
