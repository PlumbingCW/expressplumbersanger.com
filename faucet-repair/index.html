Urgent situations

When a faucet problem is an emergency

A drip can wait for a daytime visit. A faucet that will not close, a broken supply line under the sink or a tub faucet that will not shut off cannot. Close the shutoff valve under the sink, and if there is none or it will not turn, close the main water valve. Put a bucket under any drip and call.

We take emergency faucet calls 24/7 and book same-day visits when available. A supply line that bursts under a cabinet can put gallons on the floor in minutes.

Common repairs

Kitchen, bathroom and tub faucets

Dripping spout

A worn cartridge, washer or O-ring is the usual cause. A drop a second wastes thousands of gallons over a year.

Tub faucet that will not shut off

A worn cartridge or valve stem in a tub or shower. It runs and may be hard to stop. Shut the main valve and call.

Leak under the sink

A loose supply line, a failed shutoff valve or a corroded connection. These get worse fast.

Low flow

Mineral scale in the aerator or a clogged cartridge reduces flow. Hard water makes this common.

Bath fixtures

Tub and shower faucet repair

A tub spout that drips or a shower valve that will not shut off can usually be fixed by replacing a cartridge or stem behind the handle. Some valves need access through the wall. A leaking shower valve behind tile may leak inside the wall, so a plumber checks the surroundings. If you find a stain on a ceiling below, see leak detection.

Shutoff valves and supply lines

The small valves under sinks, called angle stops, and the braided lines that connect them to the faucet are cheap parts that cause a lot of floods. A valve that has not moved in ten years may leak or break when turned. A supply line that is brittle or corroded can split without warning. Replacing both during a faucet visit is a sensible precaution.

If a supply line has already burst and water is spreading, see burst pipe repair.

Local conditions

Why faucets scale up in Sanger homes

The city's 2024 water quality report shows Sanger's water averages 145.5 ppm hardness, and some tests reached 330 ppm. That hard water leaves white scale on aerators, cartridges and shower heads. Scale restricts flow and wears seals, so faucets in hard water areas drip sooner.

Unscrewing an aerator and soaking it in vinegar often restores flow. If the faucet still drips, the cartridge is likely worn. A softener protects fixtures across the house, which we cover on water softener and filtration.

Codes and sources

Standards for faucet work

According to the California Plumbing Code, which follows the UPC, new faucets and fittings must meet lead content limits for drinking water. When a faucet is replaced rather than repaired, check that the package says it meets those standards.
