Urgent signs

Slab leak signs that need a fast call

Slab leaks hide under the floor, so the signs are indirect. A warm patch on tile or wood, the sound of water running when everything is off, a damp or mildewy smell from carpet, and a sudden bill increase all point there. Cracks in walls can also appear when water undermines soil near the slab.

We treat these as emergencies. Our dispatchers book slab leak assessments 24/7, with same-day and after-hours visits when available. Shut off the main water valve while you wait, which stops the leak from growing.

Warm or hot floor

A leaking hot water line heats the concrete above it. Floors that feel warm with the heat off are a clear sign.

Sound of running water

A hissing or trickling sound with all fixtures off often comes from a pressurized pipe leaking under the slab.

Damp flooring

Wet carpet at the edge, lifting laminate or tile grout that stays damp mean water is reaching the surface.

Unexplained bill increase

A leak of just a gallon a minute is a lot of water over a month. Check the meter with everything off.

How slab leaks are found

The plumber isolates the hot and cold lines with a pressure test to learn which line is failing. Listening equipment pinpoints the spot, and thermal imaging confirms a hot water leak. Marking the floor lets the repair open the smallest possible area.

Finding the leak is the same method used for hidden leaks in walls, covered in more detail on leak detection.

Options

Repair options for a slab leak

One leak points toward spot repair. Repeated leaks point toward rerouting or repiping.

Causes

Why slab leaks start

Pipes that run under concrete face soil movement, corrosion at the outside of the pipe, abrasion where the pipe rubs against the slab, and water chemistry. A copper line can pit and fail from the outside. Older fittings fail at joints. Pressure that is too high stresses the whole system, so a plumber may check pressure at the same visit.

Sanger's municipal water averaged 145.5 ppm hardness in the city's 2024 water quality report, with results as high as 330 ppm. Hard water leaves scale inside pipes, and old scaled fittings are among the first to seep. Testing your own water is a good way to know what the lines see.

Slab leaks and your insurance

Many policies cover the water damage from a sudden slab leak, and some pay for the tear-out needed to reach the pipe, but few pay to repair the pipe itself. Policies differ, so call your agent before work begins and ask what documentation they need. A written leak report with photos helps the claim.

Safety steps

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Shut off the main valve if you hear water or feel a warm floor.
  • Turn the water heater to off if the leak is on the hot line.
  • Keep electronics off wet floors.
  • Do not drill or cut into the slab yourself.
Codes and sources

Rules for slab repairs

The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), sets the rules for pipe, fixture and water heater work in the state. Breaking a slab or rerouting lines overhead is done by a licensed plumber, and the work may need a permit. Ask your building department what applies. Plumbing work is done under a C-36 license from the Contractors State License Board, and you can look up any license on the CSLB website.
