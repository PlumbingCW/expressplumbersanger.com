Seasons

Heat in summer, fog and frost in winter

The National Weather Service's Fresno records, the closest we found because Kingsburg has no station of its own, show July highs averaging about 98 degrees. Hot months push water use up, stress irrigation lines and load water heaters that already struggle. Winter brings tule fog from late November to mid-February, and Fresno records lows at or below freezing about 19 times a year.

That freeze count is small, and a hard multi-day freeze is rare. Still, a hose bib or an exposed line on the north side of a Kingsburg house can crack on a cold night. Shutting off and draining outdoor lines in December is cheap protection.

Water supply

What Kingsburg's water does to a heater

Kingsburg draws from seven city wells and chlorinates at each well site. The city's 2025 water quality report lists hardness averaging 120 ppm, with results from 57 to 220, which is about 7 grains per gallon by the standard conversion. The report lists carbon filtration on Wells 12 and 13 and a Well 10 that is offline for 1,2,3-trichloropropane.

At that hardness a tank water heater collects scale at a steady pace. A yearly flush and an anode rod check keep it healthy. If your heater is more than ten years old and starts to leak, see emergency water heater replacement.

Permits

Kingsburg's permit rule for water heaters

Kingsburg's own permit handout says a permit is required for water heater and water softener changeouts and additions, and for adding or moving plumbing lines or fixtures. That is a clear local rule, so confirm with the Kingsburg Building Department before work starts. We follow permit requirements wherever they apply.

Around town

Neighborhoods and landmarks in Kingsburg

Kingsburg calls itself Little Sweden, and its downtown corridor holds a 22-stop interactive walking tour and the Kingsburg Historical Park. The city's housing element names neighborhoods including Bicentennial Park, Kamm and Madsen, Lincoln Square and North Kingsburg, plus areas east of Highway 99. Kingsburg High School is at 1900 18th Avenue.

The housing element says 56.6 percent of Kingsburg homes were built more than 30 years ago and 31.7 percent more than 50. Older homes near the city core are the ones that need drain and supply line attention first. The 2020 Census counted 12,380 residents.

Sewer service in Kingsburg

Kingsburg's sewage goes to the Selma-Kingsburg-Fowler County Sanitation District, whose plant sits on 550 acres 1.5 miles west of town. The city owns its local collection lines and the district operates them. For a house-side backup, our sewer line cleaning clears the line, and a camera inspection explains repeats.
