The scope

What a correct installation includes

A water heater install is more than setting a tank in place. The plumber connects the cold and hot lines, installs a shutoff valve, fits a temperature and pressure relief valve with a drain pipe to a safe location, braces the tank and checks venting and gas connections on a fuel-burning unit.

Many installs also need an expansion tank, a drain pan under the heater and new flexible connectors. These are small parts that prevent large problems, and we list them on the quote so nothing is a surprise.

  • Water supply shutoff valve and new connectors
  • Temperature and pressure relief valve with a drain pipe
  • Earthquake bracing straps on the tank
  • Expansion tank where the system calls for one
  • Gas shutoff, sediment trap and leak check on gas units
  • Venting inspected and sealed on fuel-burning units

Moving or upgrading a heater

Relocating a heater from a garage to an exterior closet, or swapping to a larger tank, changes the plumbing, the venting and often the permit picture. Tell the dispatcher what you want, and the plumber checks clearances, gas line size and electrical capacity at the visit.

A planned install is also a good time to add a leak sensor and a drain pan or to check your home's water pressure. High pressure shortens the life of any heater.

Code and permits

Rules in Sanger and California

According to California Health and Safety Code section 19211, residential water heaters must be braced against earthquake movement, so a compliant install includes strapping. Plumbing work in California must be done by a licensed contractor, and the plumbing classification is C-36. You can verify any contractor's license on the California Contractors State License Board website.

Whether your install needs a city permit depends on the city and the job. Sanger's Building Division handles building permits, and we advise checking with it before work begins.

Local conditions

Hard water and your new heater

Sanger's city wells delivered water with hardness averaging 145.5 ppm in the 2024 report, and some tests reached 330 ppm. Plan for scale. Set a yearly flush on your calendar, ask about a water softener and check the anode rod at each flush. These steps often add years to a tank.

Safety steps

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Shut off the cold water supply to the old heater.
  • Turn off the gas or the breaker.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911.
  • Keep the area around the heater clear so the plumber can work safely.
