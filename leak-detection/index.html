Urgent signs

When a leak is an emergency

A leak you can see is easy to judge. Water coming through a ceiling, a wall that is wet to the touch, a floor that feels warm or sounds like running water when everything is off are all signs of active leaks. Water damage grows by the hour, and drywall, insulation and flooring can all be ruined within a day.

Treat those as emergencies. Our dispatchers take emergency leak detection calls 24/7, including nights and weekends, and book same-day visits when available. Shut off the main water valve first, then call.

  • Close the main water shutoff, usually near the meter or where the line enters the house.
  • Turn off the water heater if the leak is hot water or you cannot find the source.
  • Switch off power to a wet room at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  • Move furniture and valuables away from the wet area and photograph the damage.
  • Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher where you see water.

Signs of a hidden leak

Many leaks announce themselves quietly. The water bill rises without a change in habits, the meter moves when every fixture is off, a patch of lawn stays soggy, paint bubbles on a wall, or pressure falls. Callers often start with a symptom, not the word leak. They describe a meter that will not stop or a bill that doubled, and those are the right words to use when you call.

Meter keeps running

With every tap and appliance off, a moving meter means water is going somewhere. A leak between the meter and the house is the usual cause.

Bill jumps

A bill much higher than last year with no change at home often traces to a toilet, an irrigation line or a buried service line.

Warm spot on the floor

A hot water line leaking under a slab can warm the floor above it. See slab leak repair.

Stains, bubbles or mold smell

Brown rings on a ceiling or bubbling paint on a wall point to a leak behind the surface, often from a supply or drain line.

Methods

How we find a leak without tearing things open

Leak detection starts with a pressure check. The plumber isolates the house, watches whether pressure drops and works out which section holds the leak. Acoustic listening devices then pick up the sound of water escaping a pipe, and a ground microphone narrows the spot. Thermal imaging shows temperature changes where hot water is escaping behind a wall or under a floor.

For some leaks a tracer gas is pushed into the system and a sensor finds it at the surface. Each tool narrows the search, so the cut in the wall or floor is small and in the right place.

Bills and meters

Meter still running, or a high water bill

Start with a simple test. Turn off every tap, the dishwasher, the washing machine and the ice maker, then look at the meter. If the dial or the low-flow indicator is moving, water is leaving the system. Note the reading, wait an hour with no use and check again. A change confirms a leak between the meter and your fixtures.

Toilets are the most common cause of a bill increase, because a flapper that leaks can run all day with no sound. Irrigation valves that stick open are next. If neither is the cause, the service line between the meter and the house is the next place to test. Our water line repair page covers buried supply lines.

Ceiling, wall and under-house leaks

A ceiling stain is rarely the leak itself. Water travels along framing before it drips, so the source may sit several feet away. Thermal imaging and moisture meters trace the wet path back to the pipe. Behind a wall, a supply line pinhole or a failed fitting at a shower valve is common.

Homes with raised foundations and a crawlspace allow access from below. A plumber can see supply and drain lines and check for wet insulation, damp soil and sagging pipe supports. Leaks under the house go unseen for months, so a musty smell or a rising bill is worth a look.

Buried lines, irrigation, yard and meter-box leaks

A leak in a buried line shows as a soft spot in the yard, grass that grows faster along a strip or water in the meter box. Irrigation line leaks behave similarly and are often found at a valve or a broken sprinkler riser. We check the meter box and the line between meter and house first because those leaks feed the bill directly.

Before any digging, call USA North 811, which needs two working days' notice in Fresno County. Pool and spa leaks follow similar logic, with a pressure test of the plumbing lines separate from a test of the shell. We do detection on the plumbing side.

Commercial leak detection

Shops, restaurants and offices have more lines and more tenants, so leak tracing takes longer and the cost of a missed leak is higher. After-hours work is possible. Tell the dispatcher about hours of operation, access and who authorizes work. Our commercial plumbing page covers larger systems.

Local conditions

Water in Sanger and what it does to pipes

Sanger gets its water from eight city wells, and the city treats it with granular activated carbon and chlorine. The 2024 water quality report shows hardness averaging 145.5 ppm and some results over 300 ppm. Hard water leaves scale that narrows pipes over decades. Scale does not cause leaks by itself, but old, scaled fittings near water heaters and valves are where we see many pinhole and seep leaks.

Chlorine in the city supply ranged from 0.92 to 1.4 ppm in the same report. That is standard disinfection, and over years chlorinated water can wear rubber parts such as toilet flappers. If your flapper is old, replacing it is a cheap first test.

Safety steps

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Shut off the main valve if water is pooling.
  • Switch off power to wet areas at the breaker only if the panel is dry and reachable.
  • Photograph damp spots and note the water meter reading.
  • Do not cut into walls or floors before a plumber has located the leak.
