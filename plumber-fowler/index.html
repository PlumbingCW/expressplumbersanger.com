Housing era

Fowler's homes and what age does to pipes

Fowler's housing element reports that 51.7 percent of the city's homes were built more than 30 years ago, and 31.3 percent more than 50 years ago. The city had about 2,035 households in 2020, and more than half of its homes are old enough to have original supply lines and drains. The housing element does not name individual neighborhoods, so we go by address.

Older supply lines fail with pinholes, and older drains collect roots. A home in the older half of town may need a leak detection visit after a bill jump or a camera visit after repeated clogs. Newer homes more often call for fixture work and water heater service.

Water supply

Fowler's water and what the city reports

The City of Fowler provides water to its residents from six groundwater wells. The 1,2,3-trichloropropane contaminant is the documented issue. A 2024-25 Fresno County Civil Grand Jury report says Fowler's annual reports have shown it in violation since 2019 and that Well 7, which supplies about a quarter of the city's water, exceeds the limit, with carbon treatment planned.

Questions about your own water belong with the Fowler Water Department. We could not find a hardness figure for Fowler, so test your tap and ask the city for its annual report. A water heater flush helps wherever the hardness lands.

Fowler's sewer and who maintains what

Fowler's sewage goes to the Selma-Kingsburg-Fowler County Sanitation District. Each member city owns its local collection lines, and the district operates and maintains them. The plant lies about 1.5 miles west of Kingsburg. For a backup inside your house, call us, and for one on the street, ask the city.

Local picture

Around Fowler

Fowler's Switch is on the state list of historical resources, registered in 1973. Fowler High School stands at 701 East Main Street, and city parks include Panzak Park on East Merced Street. The 2020 Census counted 6,700 residents, which makes Fowler the smallest of our five service towns.

Codes and sources

Rules to know in Fowler

Plumbing in Fowler follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the UPC. Ask the City of Fowler whether a repair or replacement needs a permit. Plumbing work is done under a C-36 license from the Contractors State License Board, and you can look up any license on the CSLB website.
